My head spins with the prospect of what it is I’m undergoing. A job, yes, but so far removed from what I know the idea of it churns nauseatingly in my gut. I dial my fiancé's number while my car cruises through the moon illuminated canopy road.

She speaks first. “Robert? You’re there already?”

“Nearly, the drive wasn’t as bad as we made it out to be.”

“Well that’s good.”

I pause, gauging how to phrase what I need to say next. “And Valerie, I know this all came up fast, but I really am grateful for how we’ve gone about everything. You, I mean, you’ve been nothing but supportive. I don’t think I could’ve gone here without you, what with the nights of sleep I’ve had recently.” I shake my head, attempting to clear it and focus on the narrow winding road.

“I know,” I can hear her smile. “The timing of it all was a little unfortunate, I just wish I could do more.”

“You’ve done plenty, Val. Besides, I shouldn’t be here too long right? The letter made it seem like it’d be longer than it should.”

“No, I agree.” She laughs. “It’s funny, we finally catch a break and right as we do you get an old childhood sleep disorder. Huge cosmic fuck you.”

I snort a laugh. “I don't even know.” Ahead the road bends and a new source of amber light peers from it. “Hey I think I’m pulling up to it now, I’ll call you again when I know more about what it is.”

“Okay yeah, sounds good.” A slight buzz of static before she speaks again. “I love you.”

“I love you too Valerie.”

Gravel crunches under my tires as I drive through the black iron gate to the collectors manor. The place is grand, tall and pointed, without a single window save a large circular stained glass mosaic. It depicts a colorful spiral that seems to deepen inwards as it swirls. Moonlight glints in the intricate glass. I park in the empty driveway, then reach for my work bag. Its contents shuffle within, the clanking of metal and tapping of plastic. I pull the letter from my coat pocket. Its envelope is tan, a cracked wax seal used to hold it close, the letter ‘A’ embossed in it. I open the envelope and slip out the letter inside.

To one Robert Swath,

I trust this letter reaches you well. It's to my understanding you specialize in the repair and restoration of video tapes? I’ve some in desperate need of service. However, I’m afraid I cannot deliver them. My estate is just east of Blackwater Lake, a mere hour or two away. The quantity of tapes requires your stay, I’ll have a room arranged by the time you arrive. I’m aware of the unusual circumstances of what it is I'm asking, but I assure you, you will be compensated appropriately. I’m a collector, you see, though the tapes are of a personal nature. More shall be explained at your arrival; you’ll find directions on the back. Awaiting your response eagerly,

Collector Don C. Archavich.
